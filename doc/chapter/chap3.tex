\chapter{Policy Learning}

This chapter provides a concise theoretical introduction to policy learning within the context of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and its underlying neural architectures. The reader is assumed to be a beginning graduate student or an advanced undergraduate in mathematics and computer science. As such, we assume familiarity with linear algebra, probability theory, calculus, and basic machine learning concepts such as gradient descent and multilayer perceptrons. While beneficial, prior knowledge of reinforcement learning (RL) or deep learning is not required. No original work is presented here.

For a comprehensive theoretical treatment of reinforcement learning, \cite{Sutton2018_introRL} remains the standard reference, while \cite{Winder2020_introRL} offers a concise practical perspective. Additionally, \cite{OpenAI2018_SpinningUp} provides an accessible introduction to DRL, including implementations and an overview of recent developments. Key foundational papers for our discussion include \cite{Mnih2013_DQN}, \cite{Schulman2017_PPO}, \cite{Lee2019_SetTransformer}, and \cite{Scarselli2009_GNNModel}. For background on statistical learning, we refer the reader to \cite{Hastie2009_ElementsOfStatisticalLearning}, while deep learning fundamentals are extensively covered in \cite{Goodfellow2016_DL} and \cite{Bishop2024_DL}, with practical implementations discussed in \cite{Geron2019_HandsOnML}.

The key takeaways from this chapter include the statement of a reinforcement learning problem, which we will use to express Buchberger's algorithm. An understanding of transformer and graph architectures is also necessary to understand the underlying neural structure of our policy network, which we use to learn new selection strategies.
